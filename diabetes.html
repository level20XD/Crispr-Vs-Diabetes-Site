Type 1 Diabetes

Overview

An illustration of the pancreas, the organ that produces insulin, which is attacked in Type 1 Diabetes

Source: Picryl

Diabetes is an auto-immune disease where the immune cells, which are meant to attack and provent you from foreign cells such as bacteria and viruses, attack the cells within the pancreas1. This happens when the immune system accidentally identifies the pancreatic cells as foreign1. One of the primary functions of the Pancreas is that it produces insulin, a hormone that regulates the amount of sugar in cells. This means that if the immune system attacks the pancreas, it wont be able to produce insulin, which means sugar would build up in the bloddstream1. Diabetes is mostly genetic, as people with a parent who has Type 1 Diabetes are at a higher risk of developing the disease1.

Complications

The reduced amount of insulin levels in the body can lead to several complications. Some examples include heart disease, kidney damage, eyesight impairment, and nerve damage1. Most of these complications root from problems with blood vessels, as high sugar content in the blood damages the walls of the vessels and can cause leaks or blockages2.

Current Treatments

There is currently no cure for Type 1 Diabetes, however, there are various treatments that help regulate their blood sugar levels3. The most common treatment is to manually inject insulin into your body, which can be done with a syringe or with an insulin pump3. To know how much insulin is needed, diabetics must use a device to track their blood sugar levels, such as a continuous glucose monitor3. Another important factor is the amount of carbs, fats, and protein one eats, so diabetics are advised to keep track of what they eat, since eating too much carbs will cause their blood sugar levels to spike3.
