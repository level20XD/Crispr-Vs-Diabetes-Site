What is Crispr?

Overview

Dr. Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna holding the Asturias Prize for Scientific and Technical Investigation during the 2015 awards ceremony.

Source: The Mandarin

Crispr is a gene-editing technology that can be used to change the DNA within someone's cells. DNA is the instruction set for cells, so by changing it scientists can make the cells function differently. Crispr gene-editing was first invented by two woman scientists named Dr. Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna. For their breakthrough, they both won the Asturias Prize for Scientific and Technical Investigation in 2015 and the Nobel Chemestry Prize in 20201. Because it allows for precise gene-editing, it has a lot of use in correcting genetic disorders and other diseases, and there is a lot of potential clinical used of Crispr being investigated2.

How it works:

To put it simply, Crispr gene-editing involves making cuts into the DNA so you can insert a new gene into the DNA that codes for a desired function, or replace a section of DNA with incorrect instructions with one that has the correct ones3. Crispr can also be used to cut out a section of DNA to remove an unwanted function of the cell3.

In order to make these cuts into the DNA, the Crispr procedure uses proteins called "CRISPR-associated proteins," or "Cas" proteins for short2. You can think of Cas as "Genetic scissors," as what is does is get directed to the right spot of the DNA and make cuts into it. By attaching a peice of DNA to the end of the Cas protein, the DNA peice can be inserted into the area the Cas protein cut2.

The Cas protein is guided by a "guide RNA." What the guide RNA does is it searches for a series of instructions in the DNA that matches the set of instructions it was given by the scientists2. The scientists specifically orders the instructions the same as the area they want to cut, so the guide RNA attaches to the right spot, and so the Cas protein would make the cut at the right location2.

Applications

There are several potential and current applications of Crispr gene-editing technology in medicine. As you probably know from the title of this website, there is current research being done with Crispr to create a cure for type 1 diabetes4. Some other possible applications include treating genetic disorders such as sickle cell disease, gene therapy, and Crispr even shows promise with treating HIV2.
